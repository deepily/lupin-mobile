## X1 · MAJOR · one of my own premises was wrong, and it makes B7 worse

**Chloé found that Finished Tasks does not share the row. I verified it and she is right.**

`finishedTasksTable.ts` imports neither `rowSchema` nor `rowDisclosure` — its import block (`:23-35`) pulls only `finishedTasksModel` and `taskStatusClass`, and it builds its own four-column table. The actual consumers of the shared row are three, but not the three the plan names:

```
render/templates/taskListTable.ts
render/templates/holdingAreaTable.ts
render/templates/epicBoardTable.ts      ← the real third
render/templates/taskRowDisclosed.ts    ┐
render/templates/rowDisclosure.ts       ├ the shared machinery itself
render/EpicBoardRenderer.ts             │
render/taskRowController.ts             ┘
```

(`grep -rln 'rowSchema\|rowDisclosure' multiplexer/` — Finished Tasks is absent from that list.)

**My B6 and B7 took the plan's three-pane set at face value and reasoned about the mechanism.** The mechanism argument survives intact — a shared type still does not deliver cell-for-cell identity, and a `pane:` parameter still defeats it. The *set* was wrong, and that makes B7 worse than I filed it:

§10's named guard test — *"all three task panes render the same row widget type"* — does not merely fail to catch drift. **It asserts something the web does not do**, and a builder who makes it pass has to force Finished Tasks' four `task_events` columns into a twelve-field task row. The test would drive the defect rather than catch it.

Chloé also caught the internal contradiction I walked past: §7 sends Finished Tasks to twelve fields while §8.2 gives it four columns, two sections apart.

**Revised fix, replacing the one in my B7**: the invariant binds **Task List and Holding Area**. Finished Tasks has its own row *by design*, and the plan should say so in §7 rather than leave it implied by §8.2. The guard test asserts identical ordered cell keys across those two panes, and — worth one line — asserts that Finished Tasks does **not** render `TaskRow`, so a well-meaning later hand cannot "fix the inconsistency" by unifying them.

**The Epic Board is not in this plan at all.** Not a finding against it, since Rick named five panes and that is not one of them. Worth recording because it is the pane that would make a shared-row regression visible on the web, and it is where `notifications.js:14834` says the identity is maintained *"identical cell-for-cell to the task list's row, by construction"*.

## X2 · MAJOR · a second `aria-expanded` the plan never mentions — the group header

My review covered the row's disclosure toggle, because that is what §7 describes. Chloé's grouping work surfaced a second one, in her receipt for a different finding:

> `notifications.js:12010` — `<tr class="task-group-header" role="button" tabindex="0" aria-expanded="${isCollapsed ? "false" : "true"}" aria-controls="${idSlug}">`

Verified. The web's group header is a full keyboard-operable disclosure: role, tab stop, expanded state, **and** `aria-controls` pointing at what it governs.

The plan mentions the header only as mechanics — §8.3 line 210, *"the group header toggles the collapse"* — and §7's accessibility paragraph is scoped to the row ellipsis. So the a11y half carries to one of the two disclosures and not the other.

**Why this is the more consequential of the two.** The row ellipsis hides *verbs*. The group header hides *rows*. A screen-reader user who cannot tell a collapsed group from an empty one is looking at a Task List with work silently missing from it, and nothing announces the difference. That is worse than an unreachable verb, because the user does not know to go looking.

**Fix**: the group header gets `Semantics(button: true, expanded: …, label: "<owner>, N tasks")`. The label matters as much as the flag — "collapsed" alone does not say how much is behind it. Flutter has no `aria-controls` equivalent and does not need one, since the collapsed children are absent from the tree.

## X3 · MAJOR · arms-then-confirms is silent to a screen reader, which inverts its purpose

Chloé, on the per-row destructive Submit:

> *"The per-row destructive Submit **arms then confirms on its own label**"* — `holdingAreaBatch.ts:26`, `taskVerbs.ts:116-117`, where `terminal: true` *"is the shared module's `armsTwice`. `done` is append-only, so a misclick cannot be undone"*.

She notes §7 calls the disclosed row the primary verb surface and does not carry the behaviour. The accessibility consequence is mine to add, and it is the sharpest thing in this addendum:

**In Flutter, changing a button's label in place does not re-announce it.** TalkBack's focus stays on the same node; the text under it changes and nothing is spoken. A screen-reader user taps "Won't fix", hears nothing, believes the tap missed, and taps again — which is exactly the second tap that fires the terminal action.

So arms-twice, built naively, makes a mis-tap **harder for a sighted user and no harder for a TalkBack user**. A safety mechanism that inverts for the population most likely to need it is worse than no mechanism, because the plan will record it as handled.

**Fix**: the label is a live region — `Semantics(liveRegion: true)` (`basic.dart:7343`, `semantics.dart:1555`) — or an explicit `SemanticsService.announce` on arming. Either way the armed state must be *spoken*, and the widget test should assert the announcement, not just the label change.

## X4 · agreeing with Sam's finding, disagreeing with his remedy

Sam found both Holding Area batch tooltips exist only as `title` attributes (`holdingAreaTable.ts:73`) and therefore die on a phone — taking with them the only statement anywhere that approve-all is *reversible* and won't-fix-all is *TERMINAL*. That is correct and it is important.

His remedy (c) offers two homes: *"the row's disclosure detail line, or a long-press sheet."*

**The long-press option would create the category I audited this plan for and reported empty.** My original review states that no verb in the plan sits behind a swipe, long-press or drag. A long press is technically reachable under TalkBack — double-tap-and-hold — so this is not a blocker, and I am not claiming it strands anyone. But it is a *discovery* gesture, and the content being hidden behind it is the only place the irreversible/reversible distinction is ever stated.

**Better home, and it is free**: `Semantics(label:)` and `Semantics(hint:)` on the button itself. Always announced, no discovery required, no gesture. It also composes with Sam's finding 5 — put the blast radius in the visible label, *"Won't fix all (14)"* — so the sighted user and the screen-reader user read the same thing, which is the property the `title` attribute never had even on the desktop.

⇒ **The tooltip text is not a tooltip. It is the control's name.** Treating it as decoration is what let it live somewhere a phone cannot reach.

## X5 · two independent routes to the same 48 dp floor

Worth recording because it is confirmation rather than duplication:

| | Control | Route | Floor |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sam | the two Holding Area batch buttons (~20 px as styled) | Android Material minimum; WCAG 2.2 SC 2.5.8 at 24×24 | **48 dp** |
| Rachel (B8) | the row's disclosure ellipsis | `kMinInteractiveDimension` in the vendored SDK, `material/constants.dart:27` | **48 dp** |

Different controls, different sources, same number. **And they compound**: B8 budgets 48 dp for the ellipsis on the title line, Sam's two batch buttons need 48 dp each in the group header, and X2 now wants that header to also carry an expanded state and a count. Phone width is oversubscribed in three places, not one, and they are all on the Holding Area.

## X6 · Sam's BLOCKER 2 confirms my B3 and sharpens the fix

Sam found `lib/app.dart:248-278` constructs every BLoC in an app-root `MultiBlocProvider`, so no pane can know it is foreground, and §6's battery argument has no mechanism. **Verified** — seven `BlocProvider`s at the app root, each resolving a `ServiceLocator` singleton, so a pane's BLoC outlives its route and is never disposed on navigation.

My B3 said the plan omits the BLoC layer. His receipt makes the fix concrete rather than structural: it is not enough to add a `domain/` row to §5's table. **The pane BLoCs must be route-scoped rather than app-root**, or "poll the foreground pane only" cannot be implemented at all and its named acceptance test passes vacuously.

That is a real departure from the app's existing convention, so it belongs in the plan as a decision rather than arriving as a surprise in Phase 0. The five new panes would be the first route-scoped BLoCs in this app.

Nothing Chloé or Sam found bears on the §5 directory mapping, the pre-existing 401 interceptor, the `Semantics(expanded:)` API verification, or the pull-to-refresh MINOR — and neither of them worked that ground, so their silence there is absence of coverage, not corroboration.

**Also unchanged: B8's arithmetic.** Sam's BLOCKER 1 replaces `char_budget=0` with `terse=true`, and I checked whether a terser server payload rescues the 360 dp title budget. It does not — `terse` reduces the *fields* in a row, not the length of a title, and B8's constraint is five fields plus a 48 dp target competing for 328 dp of width. If anything the two agree: both findings say the row as specified carries more than a phone can hold.

**One thing nobody checked**, stated so it does not read as passed: Rick's new ruling adds a confirm to approve-all. No reviewer has looked at that dialog's behaviour, and on Flutter an `AlertDialog` needs its focus to land on the dialog and its dismissal to return focus to the invoking control. It is routine, it is almost certainly fine, and it is unexamined.
