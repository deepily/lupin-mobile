# 2026-04-21 — FCM/APNs Push Considerations for Lupin Mobile

## Context + decision at a glance

After shipping foreground notification audio (mobile commit `94f0d77` →
amended `180a4ba`, 2026-04-21), the natural next step was extending the
pipeline to cover *off-screen* delivery — phone in pocket, app backgrounded
or killed by the OS. That led to a cross-repo bug-queue entry requesting
backend Firebase Cloud Messaging (FCM) integration.

The user pushed back with a more basic question: *what is FCM, and can we
self-host it?* Answering honestly surfaced that FCM and APNs are
OS-gatekeeper services that can't be replaced with self-hosted
infrastructure on stock consumer devices, and that the realistic
alternatives each carry significant trade-offs.

**Decision (2026-04-21): Defer off-screen notification work entirely.** The
cross-repo bug-queue entry in parent Lupin was pulled. Foreground audio
remains fully functional and production-ready. This document exists so a
future session — when off-screen delivery actually matters — can start
from the 2026-04-21 investigation rather than re-deriving it.

## Current notification audio pipeline

What shipped 2026-04-21 in `180a4ba`:

| Priority | Ding | Speech | Android channel | Sound asset |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| `low` | — | — | — | — |
| `medium` | single | — | `lupin_medium` (default importance) | `res/raw/lupin_medium.mp3` |
| `high` | single | title + message via `flutter_tts`, 300ms after ding | `lupin_high` (high importance) | `res/raw/lupin_high.mp3` |
| `urgent` | single (distinct alert tone) | title + message via `flutter_tts`, 300ms after ding | `lupin_urgent` (max importance) | `res/raw/lupin_urgent.mp3` |

The three MP3 assets are exact copies of the Lupin web client's
`src/fastapi_app/static/audio/notification-{medium,high,urgent}-priority-v2.mp3`
so audio identity is uniform across platforms. `suppress_ding` silences the
ding only (TTS still fires for high/urgent) — same behavior as the web
client's `notifications.js:5408`. Master-mute overrides everything.

**Delivery mechanism**: The notification arrives over the existing
Lupin WebSocket (`notification_queue_update` event, dispatched from
`src/cosa/rest/routers/queues.py:903,925,976` via `websocket_manager.emit_to_user_sync`).
Mobile parses the payload in `lib/app.dart` `_dispatchWsEvent`, passes the
`NotificationItem` into `NotificationsExternalUpdate`, and
`NotificationBloc._onExternalUpdate` calls
`NotificationAudioService.handleIncoming(...)`.

**Constraint**: All of the above only works *while the app is in the
foreground and the process is alive*. Moving to background is where the
pipeline falls silent.

Reference: [notification-audio-on-receipt plan doc](2026.04.21-notification-audio-on-receipt-plan.md)

## The gap: what "off-screen" actually means

Three distinct states need to be distinguished:

1. **Foreground** — app visible on screen. Process alive, WS connected,
   audio pipeline works.
2. **Backgrounded** — user swiped home or switched apps. Process usually
   alive for a few seconds to minutes; WebSocket may or may not stay
   connected depending on OS policy.
3. **Killed** — OS reclaimed the process entirely, or user force-stopped,
   or OEM battery optimization (Xiaomi, Huawei, Samsung, Oppo are notorious)
   proactively killed us. No process, no WS.

The WebSocket path only covers state 1 reliably. State 2 is partial — a
freshly-backgrounded app on lightweight stock Android may keep the socket
for a bit, but most Android OEMs will sever it within minutes to save
battery. State 3 is completely unreachable.

For the user, this means: phone-in-pocket + app-not-on-screen = zero
audible signal, no matter how "urgent" the backend tagged the notification.
Pull-to-refresh on next app open is the only way they'd find out.

**This is the gap push notifications are designed to fill.** Their
defining property is that they wake a killed app from the OS itself, not
from any application-layer connection.

## Why self-hosting isn't possible on stock devices

Both major mobile platforms deliberately centralized push delivery:

**Android**: Google Play Services (bundled in stock Android ROMs) holds a
persistent, OS-privileged socket to Google's servers. That socket is the
only channel the OS trusts for waking killed apps. Your own server cannot
request that privilege — it's granted only to Google Play Services, which
in turn only forwards messages dispatched via the FCM API using
project-scoped Google credentials.

The alternative (running your own persistent foreground service) is
possible but visible to the user via a persistent status-bar notification
and — per OEM battery policy — killed anyway on Chinese Android skins.
Discussed as Option B below.

**iOS**: APNs is the *only* delivery path. Apple signs every message route
with Apple-issued credentials and terminates the socket inside the OS
layer. Jailbreaking is the only way around it, which takes the device out
of the supported product ecosystem.

**The takeaway**: self-hosting push on stock devices isn't a missing
feature or a matter of engineering — it's a business/platform decision by
Google and Apple that makes the goal fundamentally unachievable outside
their respective clouds.

## Three realistic options

### Option A — FCM/APNs vanilla (content in payload)

**Architecture**:

```
Mobile ── FCM token registration ──> Lupin backend
Lupin backend ── messaging.Message(data={priority, message, title, ...}) ──> FCM
FCM ── OS-delivered payload ──> Mobile background handler
Mobile background handler ── flutter_local_notifications.show + flutter_tts.speak ──> User
```

**Pros**: Simplest integration. One FCM send per notification. No extra
round-trip on the mobile side. Works on iOS (via APNs) with minor payload
shape differences.

**Cons**: Google/Apple see the full notification content in transit. Even
TLS to their servers doesn't help — they decrypt and re-route on their
side. For a system that handles anything sensitive (AI assistant context,
personal agenda, job status that might mention client names), this is
probably unacceptable.

### Option A' — FCM/APNs silent relay *(recommended when revisited)*

**Architecture**:

```
Mobile ── FCM token registration ──> Lupin backend
Lupin backend ── messaging.Message(data={"notification_id": "abc"}) ──> FCM
FCM ── OS-delivered payload (no content) ──> Mobile background handler
Mobile background handler ── GET /api/notifications/abc (authenticated) ──> Lupin backend
Lupin backend ── full NotificationItem JSON ──> Mobile
Mobile background handler ── flutter_local_notifications.show + flutter_tts.speak ──> User
```

**Pros**: Content stays on Lupin's servers. Google/Apple see only the wake
signal + device token — metadata rather than payload. This is the pattern
used by Signal, WhatsApp, ProtonMail, and other privacy-conscious Android
apps. Works on both platforms.

**Cons**: One extra HTTPS round-trip per notification (~200ms typical on
good cellular, up to ~1s on bad). Still depends on Google Play Services
being installed on the user's device — Google has the routing metadata
even without content.

**Payload shape (Android data-only)**:

```json
{
  "message": {
    "token": "<device-token>",
    "data": { "notification_id": "abc-123" },
    "android": { "priority": "high" }
  }
}
```

On iOS an additional `"content-available": 1` flag is needed to wake a
killed app. Details in Apple's APNs docs.

### Option B — Android foreground service with persistent WebSocket

**Architecture**:

The mobile app registers a foreground service on boot and on login. That
service is permitted by Android to keep a persistent socket open
regardless of app state, at the cost of displaying a persistent
notification in the status bar for the service's lifetime.

**Pros**: Zero dependency on Google infrastructure. Fully self-hosted —
only Lupin's backend is in the loop. Content never leaves Lupin's servers.

**Cons**:

- Required persistent status-bar notification. OS contract. Can't be
  hidden. Visible all the time the user is logged in.
- OEM battery optimization kills foreground services on many Chinese
  Android skins despite Android's documented contract. Real-world
  reliability is 60-90% depending on device + how aggressive the user's
  battery-saver settings are.
- Measurable battery drain from keeping a TCP socket alive continuously.
- **iOS has no equivalent pattern.** iOS does not permit
  keeping a user-level WebSocket alive in the background for extended
  periods. If iOS support matters at all, Option B is non-viable for
  cross-platform parity.

### Option C — UnifiedPush / ntfy / self-hosted push ecosystems

**Architecture**:

UnifiedPush is an open protocol spec + ecosystem of "distributor" apps
(ntfy, NextPush, UP-FCM-Distributor). The user installs one of these on
their device; apps subscribe to the distributor; the distributor holds the
persistent socket to a push server (which can be self-hosted). The app
registers its push URL with Lupin's backend; Lupin's backend POSTs
notifications to the push URL; the distributor wakes the app.

**Pros**: Fully self-hostable (ntfy is an open-source server, runs on a
VPS in an hour). No Google, no Apple. Respects user privacy choices —
users who care can pick the distributor they trust.

**Cons**:

- Every user must install a distributor app before Lupin can deliver push.
  Friction.
- Install base is small — meaningful only among Android users who have
  already de-Googled or are using GrapheneOS / LineageOS with MicroG.
- iOS doesn't participate at all.
- Requires Lupin to run (or depend on) a public push relay.

Realistic niche-use option. Not a primary path for a product intended for
a broad consumer audience.

## Recommendation when this is revisited

**Option A' — FCM silent relay** is the pragmatic choice. Rationale:

1. **It works on both platforms**. Android via FCM, iOS via APNs. Option B
   and Option C can't do this.
2. **Privacy posture is acceptable.** Content stays on Lupin servers.
   Google/Apple see only routing metadata. That matches what Signal and
   WhatsApp ship with on Android, which sets an informal user-expectation
   ceiling for "acceptable Google dependency."
3. **Minimal deviation from the existing notification-audio plan.** The
   Phase 5 section of `2026.04.21-notification-audio-on-receipt-plan.md`
   documents the mobile-side implementation (deps, native config,
   `FcmService`, background handler). For Option A' the only change is:
   the background handler's first action is `GET /api/notifications/{id}`
   via the existing authenticated Dio client, *then* dispatch through
   `NotificationAudioService`. Everything else stays.
4. **Lowest-effort path to reliable off-screen delivery.** Rough estimate
   from the 2026-04-21 walkthrough: ~6 hours of backend work + ~4 hours of
   mobile work + operator time for Firebase project setup. Option B is a
   similar effort but doesn't cover iOS. Option C is more effort *and*
   gates adoption on user-device configuration.

When the trigger conditions (next section) fire and this work becomes
worth doing, the existing plan doc's Phase 5 is already most of the
implementation brief. Just strip the FCM payload to `{notification_id}`
and add the fetch step.

## Triggers to revisit this decision

The defer decision isn't permanent. Reopen this conversation if any of
these conditions fire:

1. **A user with real off-screen need.** Someone in the Lupin user base
   says "I missed a notification because my phone was in my pocket and it
   actually mattered." One such incident converts this from theoretical
   to concrete.
2. **Multi-user deployment.** Today Lupin is effectively a single-operator
   system. When user count grows and background delivery becomes a
   visible product gap vs. alternative AI assistants, the ROI shifts.
3. **iOS as a first-class target.** Option B (foreground service) is
   Android-only and would tempt us toward an asymmetric feature matrix.
   The moment iOS parity matters, Option A' becomes the only coherent
   path.
4. **Emerging off-Google push reaching real install base.** If
   UnifiedPush or a successor ever gets mainstream traction (GrapheneOS
   adoption growing, Samsung adding a distributor, EU regulation requiring
   alternative push APIs), Option C shifts from niche to viable.
5. **An active engineering session specifically scoped to off-screen
   notifications**, driven by one of the above. In that case, read this
   doc first, then re-read the Phase 5 section of the notification-audio
   plan doc, then grep the parent Lupin `bug-fix-queue.md` for any
   intervening backend work before filing a fresh bug-queue entry.

## What stays in place for now

- **Foreground audio pipeline is production-ready.** `NotificationAudioService`,
  `NotificationPreferences`, `NotificationAudioSettingsScreen`, the three
  Android channels, and the three MP3 assets are all live in the codebase
  (commit `180a4ba`).
- **Web-client parity preserved.** Mobile's priority-tier behavior matches
  `notifications.js:1354-1404,5374-5440` one-for-one, so user's mental
  model across platforms stays consistent.
- **Settings screen** exposes six user-facing toggles (master mute,
  ding × medium/high/urgent, speak × high/urgent). Those toggles are
  ready to absorb future FCM delivery without UI changes.
- **Parent Lupin bug-queue entry pulled 2026-04-21.** The
  previously-filed "Backend FCM/APNs push for off-screen notification
  audio" entry was removed from `bug-fix-queue.md` Queued section so it
  doesn't sit as a false near-term action item.
