## Q2 — Did X1 land with the right guard?

### The correction landed, cleanly

§7 line 269 opens with it, and line 279 draws the mobile conclusion:

> *"The three panes sharing `_renderRow` on web are Task List, Holding Area and Epic Board — not Finished Tasks. … Epic Board is not one of the five destinations, so on mobile only TWO panes share the row."*

With the structural reason at line 277 and the right receipts. Nothing to add.

### The guard did not

§10 test 1 (line 461) asserts the **positive** half only:

> *"Render one fixed row model through Task List and Holding Area and assert they produce the identical ordered list of cell keys."*

**Nothing anywhere asserts that Finished Tasks does *not* render `TaskRow`.** Searched the whole 619 lines: zero hits for `does NOT render`, `not render TaskRow`, or any equivalent.

**Why that matters, and it is not symmetry for its own sake.** Before the fold, the danger ran one way: §10's old test would have forced four `task_events` columns into a twelve-field row. The fold fixed that. But the fold also created the opposite pressure and left it unguarded — the plan now says these panes are deliberately different, and **nothing holds them apart**.

The specific failure: a later hand opens `finished_tasks/presentation/` , finds a bespoke row widget, and reads §7 — a section titled *"The shared row"* whose §7.1 quotes, verbatim, *"Do not give this pane a row of its own."* That sentence is about the Holding Area. Read cold by someone tidying an inconsistency, it reads as a mandate to unify. They unify, every test stays green, and Finished Tasks acquires ten fields it has no data for — `priority`, `blocked`, `accountable`, `actions` are not on a `task_event`.

**The fix is one line in the Finished Tasks widget test**: `expect( find.byType( TaskRow ), findsNothing )`, with the reason beside it. A negative assertion is the only kind that survives a well-meaning refactor, because it fails *on* the refactor rather than after it.

### X2 — partial, and the acceptance wording is unbuildable

The group header appears once, at §8.3 line 375, as a **receipt** inside a table of grouping rules:

> *"The group header is keyboard-operable — `role="button" tabindex="0" aria-expanded`"*

That records what the web does. It never says what Flutter must do, and `aria-controls` was dropped. Worse, Appendix A item 4 (line 601) turns it into acceptance wording as *"group header keyboard-operable"* — **which a phone cannot satisfy or fail, because a phone has no keyboard.** A builder cannot make that criterion true, and a reviewer cannot make it false.

**Replacement**: *"the group header is a `Semantics(button: true, expanded: …, label: "<owner>, N tasks")`; a collapsed group announces as collapsed and says how many rows it holds."* The count in the label is the part that matters — "collapsed" alone does not tell a screen-reader user whether work is hidden or absent, and the group header hides **rows**, where the row's ellipsis hides only verbs.

### X3 — dropped, and it is the one I would most want back

`liveRegion` and `announce`: **zero occurrences in 619 lines.**

§7.4 now correctly requires arms-then-confirms to carry. What is missing is the reason it fails on a phone: **in Flutter, changing a button's label in place is not re-announced.** TalkBack focus stays on the node, the text under it changes, nothing is spoken. The user believes the first tap missed and taps again — and that is the tap that fires the terminal action.

So the plan now specifies a safety mechanism that, built exactly as written, **makes a mis-tap harder for a sighted user and no harder for a TalkBack user.** It will be recorded as handled. That is strictly worse than omitting it, because the omission would at least be visible.

**Add to §7.4**: the armed label is a live region — `Semantics(liveRegion: true)` (`basic.dart:7343`, `semantics.dart:1555`) — or an explicit `SemanticsService.announce`, and the widget test asserts the announcement rather than the label change.

### X4 — inverted

§8.4 line 403 keeps Sam's original wording verbatim: *"They need a phone home — the disclosure detail line, or a long-press sheet."* `hint` appears zero times in the document.

My addendum agreed with the finding and declined that vehicle. A long press is reachable under TalkBack by double-tap-and-hold, so this strands nobody and it is not a blocker — but it is a **discovery** gesture, and what it would hide is the only statement anywhere that approve-all is reversible and won't-fix-all is terminal.

**The tooltip text is not a tooltip; it is the control's name.** `Semantics(label:)` and `Semantics(hint:)` on the button cost nothing, need no gesture, and compose with §8.4's own point 3 — put the blast radius in the visible label, *"Won't fix all (14)"* — so the sighted and the screen-reader user finally read the same thing, which the `title` attribute never achieved even on the desktop.

As it stands the fold offers, as one of two sanctioned options, the gesture-only home my review specifically flagged.
