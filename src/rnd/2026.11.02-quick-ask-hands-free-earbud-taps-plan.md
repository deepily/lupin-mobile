# Quick Ask hands-free: earbud taps

Rick wants to use Quick Ask with the phone in his pocket:

- tap once on the earbud: start recording
- tap again: stop recording
- double tap: send the transcript

Today an earbud tap already pauses and resumes a podcast. Quick Ask never sees a tap.

## Why Quick Ask can't see a tap today

| Fact | Where |
|---|---|
| Android sends an earbud press to the app's media session, and the `audio_service` package turns it into `click( button )`: one tap arrives as `MediaButton.media` and a "next" press as `MediaButton.next` | `audio_service-0.18.19/android/.../AudioService.java:974-1000` |
| The app doesn't own that media session. `just_audio_background` creates it and keeps the handler private, so app code can't intercept a press | `just_audio_background-0.0.1-beta.17/lib/just_audio_background.dart:13`, `:98` |
| The default `click` pauses if playing, else plays, which is why a tap works for podcasts and nothing else | `audio_service.dart:3089-3105` |
| The media service is declared as type `mediaPlayback` only. Recording with the screen locked also needs type `microphone` and its permission | `android/app/src/main/AndroidManifest.xml:7-10, 38-45` |

So this is new wiring. The fix has to replace the piece that owns the media session.

## The plan (approach A, recommended)

1. **The app owns the media session.**
   - Drop `just_audio_background` and call `AudioService.init` ourselves, with one app handler (`LupinMediaHandler`).
   - The handler has two modes. In podcast mode it does what the library did: play, pause, rewind 15 s, seek, stop, and the lock-screen card.
   - `JustAudioPlaybackEngine` keeps its interface (`PodcastPlaybackEngine`), so `PodcastPlayerService` and its tests don't change.
2. **Quick Ask hands-free mode.** Opening Quick Ask switches the handler to hands-free mode, and leaving Quick Ask switches it back.
   - **One tap** (`media`): if not recording, the same event as the record button (`QuickAskRecordPressed`). If recording, the same as release (`QuickAskRecordReleased`).
   - **Double tap**: the same as the small send button (`QuickAskDraftSent`).
   - Most earbuds send a double tap as "next", so `next` means send. Some send two play/pause presses instead, so two taps within 400 ms also mean send. The cost is that a single tap waits 400 ms before it acts.
   - A double tap with nothing to send does nothing except play the "nothing to send" cue.
   - If a tap arrives while recording is blocked (`canRecord` is false, e.g. a job is in flight), only the "can't do that" cue plays.
3. **Podcast and Quick Ask together.** While Quick Ask is open, taps belong to Quick Ask, and a playing podcast pauses when Quick Ask opens, so one tap means one thing. Leaving Quick Ask gives taps back to the podcast.
4. **Screen off.**
   - Add the `microphone` foreground-service type and the `FOREGROUND_SERVICE_MICROPHONE` permission to the media service.
   - Android 14 only lets the app start a microphone service while it's on screen. So hands-free mode starts its service when Quick Ask opens with the screen on, and the service keeps running after the phone is locked.
5. **Audible cues**, because he can't see the screen: short tones for recording started, recording stopped, sent, and nothing to send. They play through the existing notification audio service.
   - These tones also matter for routing. Android sends earbud presses to the app that last played audio, and the tone makes that this app.

## Tests (mine to write and run)

| Tier | What |
|---|---|
| Unit | Tap mapping: idle → record; recording → stop; `next` → send; two taps within 400 ms → send; a single tap acts after the window; a double tap with no draft sends nothing; a blocked tap only plays the cue |
| Unit | Podcast mode in the new handler: play, pause, rewind, seek, stop, and the state the lock-screen card shows |
| Widget | Opening Quick Ask enters hands-free mode, leaving it exits; a fake tap stream drives the bloc events |
| Gate | Full suite plus `tool/merge_gate.py` |
| Phone (Rick, once) | Real earbuds, phone locked in a pocket: tap, tap, double tap sends. Then a podcast from the lock screen still pauses and resumes with a tap. A widget test can't produce a real Bluetooth button press, so this one needs the phone |

## Risks

- **Podcast regression.** Replacing the library rewrites the lock-screen and earbud control for podcasts. Unit tests cover the handler, but the lock-screen card itself needs his phone check.
- **Routing on his Android version.** Whether the press reaches the app while locked depends on Android's choice of media session. The cue tone is the mitigation; his phone check is the proof.
- **Earbud firmware.** If his earbuds send a double tap as something other than "next" or two presses, a double tap won't send. The fix then is one line, once we know which key his earbuds send.

## Approach B (smaller first step)

The same handler and tap mapping, but **screen on only**: no microphone service, and no locked-screen recording yet. This is a smaller first APK with less Android risk, but it isn't yet the phone-in-pocket use he described. The locked-screen part would follow as a second step.
